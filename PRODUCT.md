# Product

**User.** A parent deciding whether and how a teenager should use an app; a teacher or counsellor answering parents; a researcher or journalist comparing platforms' design choices.

**Painful job.** "What is this app set to by default for my teen: who can see them, who can message them, what are they shown, when does it go quiet at night, and what can I control?" The answers sit in help centers, safety centers, transparency pages and launch announcements that differ by platform and change without notice.

**Promise.** One page per platform with seven plain answers, each quoted from the platform's own documentation with a link, plus how its feed ranks content. Re-checked weekly, changes logged.

**Wedge.** Search ("instagram teen account settings", "tiktok screen time default teens", "youtube autoplay off for teens", "snapchat who can message my teen") and AI answer engines that cite dated, quoted sources. Embeddable summary badges and the change feed bring links and repeat visits.

**Why now.** Platforms keep adding teen defaults (Instagram Teen Accounts, TikTok's 60-minute default, YouTube's bedtime reminders) and state and national rules keep changing what is required. Parents read about changes in the news but can't easily see the current state side by side.

**Activation metric.** A visitor follows a quote to the platform's own document (`source-click`). Instrumented; counting turns on with the GoatCounter key.

**First revenue event.** None on Pages (non-commercial). The marked slot is a support link (OPERATIONS.md, key 2). Plausible later: a licensed dataset or change alerts for schools and researchers, hosted elsewhere.

**What v1 deliberately won't do.** Profile CEOs or any person, rate apps as safe or unsafe, give medical or parenting advice, test apps, or read pages that robots.txt closes to automated readers.
